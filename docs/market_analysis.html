focusstack: where it actually stands against the market

An honest competitive and market analysis of the focusstack research engine (per-band edge-aware multi-focus fusion, Python/OpenCV, CLI-only, CPU-only) against professional, free, and in-camera focus-stacking alternatives, as of July 2026.

All prices verified on vendor pages July 2026 and linked inline. Every red cell in the comparison table is a real gap, including ours.

Executive summary

As an algorithm, focusstack is genuinely competitive at the research frontier; as a product, it is not shippable today, and the market it would ship into is small, cheap, and being commoditized from below.

The engine's core fusion (perband) is validated against ground truth in six regimes and its gated contour-reconstruction specialist — plus a demonstrated willingness to withdraw a branch when a harder hallucination audit overturns its gate — has no equivalent in any commercial or open-source stacker we found. But: it has no GUI, no RAW support, an 8-bit pipeline, no retouching workflow, unresolved deep-stack handheld alignment, and no benchmark against Helicon or Zerene outputs — only against our own reimplementations of their algorithm families. Meanwhile the paid market tops out at a $289 one-time license, Affinity's focus merge went free under Canva, and OM System cameras stack in-body. The realistic paths are open-source credibility, an embeddable engine/SDK, or a microscopy/machine-vision vertical — not a consumer app.

1. What we are, honestly

What exists: a research-grade CLI (focusstack shots/*.jpg -o sharp.png) built on Python 3 + OpenCV, CPU-only. The scientific core is real and unusually well-evidenced:

Validated strengths

  • Per-band edge-aware fusion (perband) — multi-scale decision and halo-free reconstruction; GT-SSIM 0.9918 on Real-MFF (710 real pairs) and 0.9021 at 3072 px, beating classical pyramid and guided-blend baselines at both resolutions, with the lead widening under honest occlusion physics.
  • Confidence hardening — one mechanism that rescues thin bright structures and rejects defocus-spread bleed.
  • One gated boundary specialist (contour reconstruction). The former veil specialist is safety-disabled after a realistic-object/native-resolution audit overturned its narrow held-out result; this is a strength of the evidence process, not a second capability.
  • GT-calibrated multi-scale metrics — we fixed the standard QAB/F metric's high-res collapse and validated the composite against ground truth (+0.785/+0.869 Spearman at low/high res).
  • Exposure/WB-drift correction, default-on — recovers −0.025 SSIM of damage to −0.002, provably near-identity on clean stacks.
  • Real-data validation — real fluorescence-microscopy z-stacks (BBBC006) and 13 real handheld phone sweeps, plus a depth-from-focus byproduct (--depth-out).
  • Content-aware focus operators, an explicit speed/quality tradeoff via --fast (~1.5× at a measured −0.005 to −0.025 GT-SSIM), and an honest research record (54 dated findings incl. rigorous negatives and a shipped-feature withdrawal).

Known gaps (our red cells)

  • No GUI. Every competitor below, including the free ones, has one.
  • No RAW, 8-bit only. The pipeline is uint8 JPEG/PNG/TIFF in, 8-bit out. Pros shoot RAW and expect 16-bit; Helicon does RAW-in-DNG-out.
  • No retouching workflow. The single feature pros most cite when choosing Zerene/Helicon. Gated contour reconstruction is low-recall and does not replace manual correction.
  • CPU-only Python; no GPU. The ~1.5× speed preset has a measured quality cost. No head-to-head timing exists vs Helicon's OpenCL engine, and we should assume we lose badly on 100-frame 45 MP stacks.
  • Handheld deep-stack alignment is an open wall — single-pass ECC to a middle frame; large-motion sweeps ghost (tracked as frontier item #7/#8).
  • Deep stacks validated to N=8; macro pros routinely shoot 50–200 frames.
  • No benchmark against commercial tools' actual output. Our GT wins are vs our own implementations of the classical method families, not vs Helicon/Zerene binaries.
  • Real-photographic GT validation still pending (the iPhone-12 dataset is the promotion gate), single-maintainer research code, no installer, no support.

3. What is genuinely differentiated

Three things in this repo do not exist elsewhere in the focus-stacking market, as far as our research found:

3.1 Gated contour correction with evidence-driven refusal

Every stacker fails at depth boundaries sometimes; the industry's universal answer is manual retouching (Zerene's flagship workflow, Helicon's brush tools). Our answer is structural but narrower: contour reconstruction behind a gate trained on the actual quality outcome of firing. The veil branch originally used the same recipe, but F54 showed that a gate inherits its factory and labels' blind spots: the branch is now safety-disabled. No competitor we found publishes this kind of automated boundary-correction evidence or a comparable feature-withdrawal audit. This is a real research contribution, not a do-no-harm guarantee and not yet a replacement for retouching.

3.2 GT-calibrated honesty as a feature

The evidence culture — six validation regimes, metrics themselves validated against ground truth, negatives published (oracle de-veiling loses; spread import doesn't materialize) — is unique in this market. Commercial tools compete on reputation and forum lore; academic MFIF papers compete on benchmark tables that rarely survive contact with high resolution. A GT-validated, per-regime evidence table is a credibility asset if we ever publish or open-source.

3.3 The multi-scale decision insight (perband)

The specific combination — edge-aware, confidence-hardened decision made independently per pyramid band — resolved the low-res/high-res ranking reversals that break single-scale methods, with zero tuned scale parameters. Aimonen's tool uses complex wavelets (also multi-scale) but with no edge-aware decision or confidence hardening and no published quality evaluation; commercial vendors don't disclose their algorithms. Caveat, again: we have never scored Helicon's or Zerene's actual output against our GT benchmarks. Doing so is cheap and should precede any external quality claim.

4. What blocks productization

Hard blockers (table stakes we lack)

  • 16-bit + RAW pipeline. An 8-bit engine is disqualifying for the entire professional segment. This is also the deepest change — every stage assumes uint8.
  • GUI with before/after and retouch. The buying population is photographers, not CLI users.
  • Deep-stack alignment. 50–200 frame handheld/rail stacks are the professional workload; our alignment is the known open wall.
  • Speed. Python/CPU; the measured 1.5× preset costs roughly 0.005–0.025 GT-SSIM, while competitors are C++/GPU.

Soft blockers (trust and operations)

  • Packaging & platform: no installer and no Windows/macOS builds.
  • Support: single maintainer; Zerene's author-answers-email support is part of what its $289 buys.
  • Real-photographic GT validation is still pending (iPhone-12 gate) — our own standards say the strongest claims aren't yet earned.
  • Head-to-head benchmark vs Helicon/Zerene output — absent, and required before any competitive quality claim.

5.2 Who actually buys

We found no credible market-size report specific to focus-stacking software, and we decline to invent one. (Generic "market research" mills sell numbers for this; none survive scrutiny.) The honest sizing is qualitative: this is a niche utility market measured in tens of thousands of enthusiast/professional seats, dominated by two lifestyle-scale vendors (Helicon Soft, Zerene Systems — the latter essentially one person). Segments, in rough order of willingness to pay:

Macro / insect / nature photographers

  • The core enthusiast market; communities like photomacrography.net run detailed Zerene-vs-Helicon comparisons.
  • Shoot 20–200+ frame stacks on rails; care about crossing-bristle detail and retouching.
  • Price-anchored at $89–289 one-time. Reviews frame these tools as the quality benchmarks.

Product / jewelry / watch e-commerce

  • Focus stacking is standard practice for jewelry and product photography; shallow DoF at close range makes it mandatory.
  • Increasingly bought as part of automated capture systems (Orbitvu, PackshotCreator, Iconasys turntables) — the stacker is a component, not the product.
  • Values batch throughput and consistency: the segment most compatible with a headless engine.

Microscopy labs, museums, archives

  • EDOF is a routine need in z-stack microscopy; vendors sell it as software modules (e.g. PROMICRA Deep Focus) or built into microscope suites.
  • Zerene's license tiers explicitly name museums and funded projects — evidence institutions are paying customers.
  • Values reproducibility, batch CLI, documented behavior — our actual strengths; also where our only real-optics validation lives (BBBC006).

Industrial / machine vision (adjacent)

  • Focus stacking ships inside AOI inspection systems (e.g. Basler's integrated EDOF use case) for PCB/semiconductor inspection.
  • B2B component licensing, not shrink-wrap: determinism and auditable failure matter — a natural fit for this evidence discipline, but it demands C++/GPU-grade speed we don't have.

5.3 The squeeze from below

  • Free desktop tier is now genuinely good. Affinity's focus merge — previously a $70 one-time app and a credible Photoshop rival — is now free under Canva, with focus stacking included in the free tier.
  • Cameras stack in-body. OM System bodies composite bracketed frames in-camera; Canon offers depth compositing on recent R-series bodies; Nikon/Fuji auto-bracket for external stacking. The casual end of the market never opens a desktop stacker.
  • Smartphones absorb the bottom. Computational photography delivers "everything in focus" by default for snapshots.
  • What remains willing to pay is precisely the deep-stack professional segment — whose top requirements (retouching, RAW/16-bit, speed on 100+ frames, robust alignment) map one-to-one onto our four biggest gaps.

6. Realistic positioning options

  1. Open-source research engine Most realistic now
    Release as the best-evidenced OSS stacker: GT-validated quality, gated contour correction, the depth-from-focus output, and the research record as documentation. Competes with focus-stack/ChimpStackr on evidence rather than features. Costs: 16-bit support is near-mandatory even here. Payoff is credibility, users, contributors, and real-world stacks — not revenue. This also de-risks every other option.
  2. Embeddable engine / SDK for people who already own the workflow Best economics, if we close speed + 16-bit
    License the fusion core to automated product-photography systems (Orbitvu/PackshotCreator/Iconasys class), microscopy software vendors (PROMICRA class), or machine-vision integrators (Basler-style AOI). Sidesteps our GUI/RAW/retouch gaps entirely — the customer owns capture and UI; we provide a deterministic, deterministic, audit-friendly fusion core. Blockers: C++/GPU-grade throughput, 16-bit, and at least one head-to-head benchmark. This is the only option where our differentiation converts to money at more than hobby scale.
  3. Niche vertical: microscopy / scientific EDOF Plausible, slow
    Our only real-optics validation is microscopy; labs value batch CLI, reproducibility, and documented behavior over GUIs. But incumbents ship EDOF inside microscope-vendor suites, and reaching labs means integrations (Bio-Formats/OME-TIFF, proprietary stage formats) plus institutional sales cycles. Viable as a focused wedge, not a quick win.
  4. Prosumer desktop app vs Helicon/Zerene Avoid
    We would enter a two-decade-incumbent, reputation-driven market, missing its four table-stakes features, with a price ceiling of $289 one-time, a free Affinity below, and in-camera stacking eroding the base. Even executed perfectly this is a lifestyle business, and we would start years behind on the features that decide purchases.

7. Bottom line

Where we beat the professionals: evidence and architecture. Nobody else publishes GT-referenced quality across regimes; nobody else we found pairs automated contour correction with this level of outcome auditing; the per-band decision resolves a real failure mode (resolution-dependent ranking reversals) that classical pyramid tools — and by their halos, likely some commercial modes — still exhibit.

Where we lose: everything the buyer touches. GUI, RAW, 16-bit, retouching, deep-stack alignment, speed, packaging, support — and we have never scored our output against Helicon's or Zerene's actual binaries.

What the market says: the specialist price ceiling is $289 one-time; the free floor now includes a professional-grade GUI app (Affinity); capture hardware is absorbing the casual segment. The differentiated, defensible path is the engine — open-sourced for credibility, then embedded where someone else owns the camera and the buttons.
